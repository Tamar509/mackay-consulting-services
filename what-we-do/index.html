What we do

Three practice areas, designed to be bought together.

Our practice areas are separable, and clients often commission one. Their value, however, is cumulative: a statutory instrument is stronger when the person drafting it knows how the registry that administers it will be built, and an evaluation is sharper when its indicators were written into the regulation in the first place.

01

Law and regulatory reform

Legislation is the point at which policy becomes enforceable. It is also the point at which good policy is most often lost — to ambiguous drafting, to powers that no institution has the capacity to exercise, or to commencement provisions that quietly never arrive.

We work on primary and subsidiary legislation from policy conception through to enactment and commencement. That includes preparing drafting instructions to law officers in the form the Law Officers Department expects, drafting or reviewing bills and statutory instruments, and carrying out regulatory impact assessment so that the costs of compliance, the burden on small enterprises and the administrative capacity required are all on the table before a measure is laid.

We pay particular attention to the machinery: appointment and removal provisions, appeal routes, fee-setting powers, transitional arrangements, and the reporting duties that make a regulator accountable to Parliament. These are the clauses that determine whether an institution functions.

Typical assignments

  • Legislative drafting: bills, amendment bills and consolidations
  • Statutory instruments, regulations, rules and orders
  • Drafting instructions to law officers
  • Regulatory impact assessment and cost of compliance analysis
  • Legal and regulatory gap analysis against treaty or ECOWAS obligations
  • Regulatory design for new or restructured agencies
  • Licensing, enforcement and appeals frameworks
  • Public and stakeholder consultation on draft instruments
  • Legislative scrutiny support to committees and law reform bodies
02

Digital government and data governance

Digitalisation fails in the public sector for a predictable reason: the system is specified as a technology project when it is in fact a restatement of an institution’s legal process. A licensing platform is the Licensing Act rendered as software. If the two disagree, the software wins in practice and the statute wins in court.

We specify e-governance systems from the mandate outwards. We map the statutory process, identify where discretion legally sits, redesign the workflow, and write functional and non-functional requirements that a procurement can be run against and a vendor can be held to. We do not build the software; we make sure the right software gets built and that the institution can supervise the people building it.

On data protection and data governance we advise on lawful basis, data sharing between agencies, retention and disposal, records of processing, data protection impact assessment, breach procedures and the practical compliance obligations that follow from Sierra Leonean and comparable regional data protection law.

Typical assignments

  • Digitalisation of institutional and regulatory processes
  • E-governance strategy and service design
  • Business process mapping and re-engineering
  • Functional and technical systems specification for procurement
  • Digital readiness and institutional capability assessment
  • Data protection compliance programmes and DPIAs
  • Inter-agency data sharing agreements and governance frameworks
  • Records management, retention and disposal policy
  • Vendor evaluation support and acceptance criteria
03

Inclusive economic development

Inclusion is a budgeting and regulatory question before it is a programming question. A ministry that cannot disaggregate its own expenditure cannot demonstrate who benefited from it, and a credit regulation drafted around salaried borrowers will exclude the traders and smallholders who make up most of the economy.

We support ministries of finance, sector ministries and development partners on gender-responsive budgeting — budget call circulars, gender budget statements, expenditure tagging, and the analytical work that makes a gender marker mean something at audit.

On MSME development and access to finance we work on the constraints that sit between a viable enterprise and a lender: collateral registries, movable asset finance, credit reporting, agent banking and digital financial services rules, and the design of guarantee and on-lending facilities. On youth and adolescent programming we support skills, transition-to-work and adolescent health and protection initiatives, with an emphasis on realistic targeting and measurable outcomes.

Typical assignments

  • Gender-responsive budgeting: policy, guidelines and rollout
  • Gender and social inclusion analysis and action plans
  • MSME policy, strategy and regulatory review
  • Access to finance diagnostics and market assessments
  • Collateral registry, credit reporting and movable asset finance reform
  • Digital financial services and agent banking frameworks
  • Youth employment and adolescent programming design
  • Beneficiary targeting and eligibility design
  • Programme theory of change and results frameworks

Underpinning all three

Research, evaluation and institutional capacity building

Research and evaluation

Applied policy research, baseline and endline studies, mixed-methods data collection, and monitoring and evaluation designed to donor standards — theories of change, logframes and results frameworks, process and outcome evaluation, and value-for-money analysis. We write evaluations that survive contact with an independent quality assurance panel.

Institutional capacity building

Capacity assessment, structured training for drafters, regulators and programme staff, coaching and secondment-style support, and the manuals, templates and standard operating procedures that let a reform outlive the engagement. Our default position is that the client team should be able to run the process without us at the end of it.

Most of our conversations begin with a problem rather than a terms of reference. If you are at that stage, we are happy to think it through with you.
